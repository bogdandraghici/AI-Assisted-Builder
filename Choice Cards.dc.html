Change is a disclosure, not a commit — and neither is the row. It opens with the selection on the answer already there, so Apply offers nothing until you move it, and what you move is a selection: leave it open, press Escape, click the handle again, and the answer you had is the answer you keep. Same two acts as a card that is asking, because it is the same decision being made twice.
Something else — I will tell you carries no answer of mine, so taking it opens a field under the row and the answer is the one you write. Same two acts as every other row — the row chooses, Apply commits — and the same one test: an empty field has nothing in it to commit, and words that say what they already said are the answer that is already there. Enter is Apply while Apply is live. Your words are then the whole of the settled sentence, and they read back into the field if you come to change it.
The label over the list is a rule, not a state. What I chose for you is the queue of things you never answered, so any row you take on the second card empties it into Answered by you — including the one I proposed. Agreeing with me is still answering; the way to leave it mine is to leave it alone.
No orange anywhere. The plan behind an open card is identical to the plan behind a closed one — nothing dashed, nothing struck — and it counts nowhere as waiting on you. Both cards may be open at once, because there is no queue for something nobody is waiting on.

Two acts on a card that asks you to choose: Apply, then Comment, in that order because the first is the one it wants. Apply is withdrawn until a row is chosen — pointer and keyboard together, never a live control that looks dead. Every card that asks you to choose has it, settled ones included: answering changes the plan and the card is drawing what that would do, so the row and the drawing get to be read against each other before it lands. Nothing to commit is one test on both grounds — the selection is still the row that was already taken, which on a card that has never been answered is no row at all. A card gets a third only where it has a way out its rows do not cover, like the file card's Go on without it. The fourth here is not disabled but withdrawn: in the plan behind an open step, pointer-events and tabindex go together.
