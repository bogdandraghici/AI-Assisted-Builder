The choice cards
Every decision in a plan arrives as one card. It has two grounds and nothing in between: the orange wash means this is waiting on your reply, and the quiet outlined card means it is settled. Which reply is owed comes from the glyph and the word — never from the colour, because a second orange would make the two kinds of waiting one mark. Cards drawn on this sheet carry the product's own values, read out of Conversational Builder v2.

Settled is one register: #101820 on #1c2530, no wash, no glyph, and the sentence in the plan's own voice — the bold lead-in is what was decided, the rest is what it came to.
The eyebrow is there only when the card needs to say whose answer it is: Answered by you sits in the list of things that were owed. Under What I chose for you the section label already says it, so the card drops the eyebrow rather than repeat it.
The third shape holds no decision at all — the settings a step runs on, as a sentence with the values dashed-underlined in mono. Editing in place is the whole affordance; there is nothing to agree to.
One verb for everything settled, and it is Change. Not Edit, not Revisit, not Reopen — the builder is never asked to work out which of four words leads back to the same panel. It rides the sentence, not the eyebrow, because the sentence is what it changes.
