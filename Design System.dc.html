Base 4, and the ramp is used unevenly on purpose: 12 and 16 carry almost all padding, in a panel and in a table row alike. Nothing sits between two steps — a 14 or a 20 in the source is a mistake, not a nuance.
Radius climbs with the size of the thing it holds — 2 for a tick or dot, 4 for a branch chip, 6 for an icon button, 8 for a button or inline card, 12 for a panel, 99 for anything round by nature. Nothing between.
Canvas 1440 × 900, shell radius 12. Two panes per screen: 440 conversation · fluid plan on 01, and 280 plan · fluid step on 02. The elastic column always holds the artifact, and prose inside it is capped at 800 so a line stays readable.

07 — The signature gesture, the step rail
Every resource carries a five-slot rail — one slot per plan step, on a dashed baseline. A raised tick means that step claims it.
So the non-1:1 relationship is readable standing still: two ticks means shared, an empty rail means the resource belongs to the project and no step, and a step with too many claims is visibly doing too much. Blue ticks are claims on something that exists; muted ticks are claims on something only spoken about.
48 × 12 · pitch 10 · tick 8 × 11 · baseline 1px dashed
One gesture. No connector lines, no nesting, no lie.
The plan itself carries the other one — a 2px spine down the left with a 20px node per step. The spine is solid where the plan is real and dashed the moment it stops being, so how far the build has actually got is visible without reading a word.
Four nodes, and status is never said twice: a green disc with a tick is built, an orange disc with a warning is being argued about, a dashed ring is not built, and a ring holding a person is waiting on you. Inside a single step the spine is dropped — there is no sequence left to draw — and the rail groups by state instead, under mono headers tinted to the state they name. Because the node carries it, a step needs no badge — the one chip left on a screen is the exception the node is already pointing at.
